Causal loop diagrams describe hypothesized relationships between variables and show how reinforcing and balancing feedback can shape system behaviour. UNDP presents them as a way to explore complex interventions and theories of change.[^s1]

## How to use

Define a focused question, name variables that can increase or decrease, and give each directed relationship a polarity. Trace complete loops, distinguish reinforcing from balancing feedback, and record the reasoning and evidence behind each link. Revisit the map with stakeholders.

## Evidence and limitations

A signed arrow is a causal hypothesis, not proof of an effect. Qualitative loops do not estimate effect sizes or run a calibrated simulation. Avoid treating a dense map as a comprehensive model.

## Suggested project use

For [UNDP sensemaking](../projects/undp-sensemaking.md), use a map to discuss competing intervention mechanisms alongside document citations. For MinCiencia, explore feedback between capabilities, funding and adoption as explicit hypotheses.

## Relationships

Documented by [UNDP](../organizations/undp.md); complements [participatory mapping](participatory-systems-mapping.md). The [illustrated handbook](../readings/causal-loop-handbook.md) explains polarity and archetypes. [React Flow](../software/react-flow.md) could supply an editor, not the causal reasoning.
