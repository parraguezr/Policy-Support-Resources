## What it is

A 44-page training slide deck hosted by the World Bank, presented by Boris F. Ochoa-Tocachi of ATUK Consultoría Estratégica. It teaches how to communicate ecosystem and landscape assessment results to policy audiences. The title slide does not state a publication date. Page references below use PDF positions, including the blank first page, rather than the printed slide numbers.[^pdf]

## Summary

The deck treats a policy brief as a concise, accessible bridge from research to a policy decision. It distinguishes explaining research from addressing a policy problem, weighing options and proposing action for a defined audience (PDF pp. 6–10).[^pdf]

Planning starts with purpose, audience, content and structure. Writers should identify the decision and readers' needs, select relevant evidence, and make the practical policy message easy to find (pp. 11–18).[^pdf]

Its suggested structure combines an executive summary, introduction and problem context, examination of findings and options, and policy recommendations. Guidance covers why the problem matters, links between evidence and options, benefits and trade-offs, and feasible next steps. Appendices can carry supporting detail (pp. 19–35).[^pdf]

The closing slides address titles, headings, sidebars, purposeful graphics and captions, then revision and presentation. Suggested checks include a short verbal pitch, removing jargon and obtaining feedback from a non-specialist colleague. Presentations should prioritize key messages and allow questions (pp. 36–42).[^pdf]

## Evidence and limitations

This is practical communication guidance, with examples and further-reading links; it does not evaluate whether the suggested format improves policy outcomes. Its length and layout suggestions are teaching recommendations, not universal requirements. Linked further reading has not been independently reviewed for this entry.
