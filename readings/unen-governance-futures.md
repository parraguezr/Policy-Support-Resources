## Summary

The brief examines how governance at global, national and local levels could evolve by 2040. It argues for institutions that anticipate change, coordinate across levels and sustain inclusive participation amid technological, geopolitical and social disruption (p. 1).[^pdf]

Its foresight exercise combines PESTLE driver mapping, a survey administered to 23 UN agencies, horizon scanning, network analysis and facilitated scenario development. The dashboard and AI assistant support exploration of survey evidence (pp. 11–13).[^pdf]

Four forces organize the analysis: geopolitical shifts, technology, changing social behavior and non-state actors. Four baseline scenarios are contrasted with four radical scenario pairs, including democratic versus authoritarian uses of AI, renewed participation versus disorder, and inclusive decentralization versus state failure (pp. 14–18).[^pdf]

The proposed responses include accountable AI governance, participation beyond elections, stronger local institutions and foresight capacity. Separate action tables address the UN system, Member States and engagement with non-state actors; advisory work, training and knowledge exchange support implementation (pp. 19–22).[^pdf]

## Evidence and limitations

These are exploratory futures and proposed actions, not predictions or demonstrated effects. The report's disclaimer separates the authors' views from official UN policy (p. 22).[^pdf] Referenced studies were not independently revalidated.
